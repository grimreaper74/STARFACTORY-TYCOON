# Re-audit, 2026-08-27 — v001

Greg asked for a fresh audit after two days of building. This is it, with
one caveat stated up front.

**I am the least neutral reader of this code.** The first audit's value was
independent eyes; I wrote nearly everything being audited here, so I am
exactly the person likely to share its blind spots. I compensated by chasing
evidence — greps, round-trip tests, live runs — rather than reading my own
work and agreeing with it. A genuinely independent pass would still be worth
running, and I would not treat this as a substitute.

## Findings

### 1. Two days of new save state had no coverage — clean, now covered

Roughly eight fields were added to the save: workmanship defects, rework
owing, finished-but-unsold, a contract's customer, livery and deadline, and
the per-delivery credit both progression ladders keep. **Not one had a line
of save coverage.**

They all round-trip correctly — the pipeline captures whole snapshots per
authority, so new fields ride along automatically. That is a good design
holding up, not something anyone verified. It is verified now: a save with
every field set to a non-default value, the live state wrecked afterwards,
reloaded, and asserted field by field — including that the credit high-water
marks return, so a reload cannot pay for the same delivery twice.

**No bug. The risk was real and is now closed.**

### 2. The build menu filed things where nobody would look — real, fixed

It grouped by a hardcoded list of ids, so anything added after that list was
written fell through to CRAFTING CHAIN. The **delivery dock** I added
yesterday landed there — and so had the **power station** and the
**sub-assembly hall** long before me. Three buildings, none of which craft
anything, sitting under crafting.

Grouping is derived from what a definition *is* now: a bigger mark is a heavy
mark, a stage-servicing station is a line station, something with recipes is a
parts machine, everything else is infrastructure. A test walks the whole
catalogue, so the next building added cannot be misfiled either.

### 3. Finished stock was invisible — real, fixed

`GetStockedCraftCount` had no consumer outside its own test. The game knew a
craft was built and unsold; the player had no way to find out. Same shape as
every other bug this week: **the simulation knowing something it never says.**

It matters more than it sounds — a craft in stock fills the next matching
order instantly, so knowing you have one is the difference between an offer
being work and an offer being free money, and the player was choosing between
offers without it.

### 4. Insolvency — sound, no change

A player who spends everything is not stuck: removing a building refunds half
its cost, so there is always a way back. Checked rather than assumed.

### 5. Localisation — clean

No raw player-facing strings in the widgets; everything added over two days
goes through LOCTEXT. Authority-side refusal strings stay English and
greppable, which is the documented convention.

The one exception worth your eye: **customer display names are plain strings
in a catalogue** — proper nouns, so arguably right, but they will not
translate. Placeholders anyway, pending your naming.
