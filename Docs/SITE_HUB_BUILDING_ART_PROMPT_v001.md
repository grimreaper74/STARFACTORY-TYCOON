## THE BRIEF

I need twelve building illustrations for the overview screen of a
factory-management game called **Star Factory Tycoon**. The player runs a
plant that manufactures small spacecraft to order. The overview is a
single painted site seen from above at a fixed angle; each building is a
separate image dropped onto it, and clicking one enters that building.
Buildings the player has not unlocked yet are shown dimmed with a
padlock, so all twelve must read clearly in a dimmed, desaturated state
as well as a lit one.

### The look, in one line

**Clean futuristic industrial — not grimy sci-fi.** Pale industrial
surfaces, graphite structure, strong clean light, crisp edges. Think a
brand-new automated plant on a prepared site: nothing rusted, nothing
weathered, nothing leaking steam. No cyberpunk, no neon, no dust, no
decay, no alien organic shapes.

### Hard rules — these are not preferences

1. **No text of any kind.** No letters, numbers, words, signage, logos,
   placards, or numerals on any surface. The game ships translated and
   baked text cannot be localised. This includes fake or alien writing.
2. **Nothing is human-scaled, because nothing here is handled by a
   person.** The whole site is worked by drones. So: no person-sized
   doors, no stairs, no ladders, no handrails, no walkways, no balconies,
   no windows that imply occupied floors, no car parks, no fire exits.
   Openings are sized for machines and drones. This is the single most
   common mistake in briefs like this — please read it twice.
3. **No surface may be both bright and saturated.** Colour is either
   pale or dark, never vivid. This is the rule that keeps the world from
   fighting the interface.
4. Keep every building **reading at small size** — roughly 300 px wide on
   screen. Silhouette does the work; fine surface detail is lost.

### Palette — use these exact values

- Structure, frames, ribs, dark masses: **#4A4D50** (graphite)
- Main building surfaces and cladding: **#D6D2CB** (pale housing)
- Ground, aprons, hardstanding: **#C9C5BE** (concrete)
- Warm metal accents, edge strips only, never a whole body: **#A87334**
- Lit indicators and emissive trim: **#BFE4FF** (cool working blue)
- Ground hazard striping only, 45°, always paired with near-black
  **#23211F**: **#C9A21C**

**Emissive trim is the signature of this architecture** — thin cool-blue
light lines along roof edges, door thresholds and structural seams. Use
it sparingly and consistently on every building so they read as one
estate. Warm amber appears only as narrow edge strips, never as a
building's main colour.

### View and format — consistency matters more than any single image

- One **fixed three-quarter view from above**, roughly 40° elevation,
  identical for all twelve. They sit on the same map, so a mismatched
  angle ruins the set.
- **Identical light direction** on all twelve — key from the upper left,
  soft fill, one soft contact shadow beneath each building.
- **Transparent background.** No ground plane, no scenery, no sky. Just
  the building and its contact shadow.
- Draw each to **consistent relative scale** using the real footprints
  given below, so a 120 m building is visibly smaller than a 180 m one.
- Deliver each as a separate **PNG with alpha**, square canvas, building
  centred with a little margin.

### Scale anchors — non-human ones

Because there are no people, convey size with the machines instead:
cargo drones (about 3 m across), the delivery vehicles at a goods door,
and for the two ship buildings, a small spacecraft roughly 12 m long.
Include one or two of these near each building where it helps read the
scale. Keep them consistent between images.

## THE TWELVE BUILDINGS

Available from the start:

1. **Ship factory** — 180 × 180 m. The main assembly hall where the
   craft are built along a line under a gantry crane. Big clear-span
   shed, ribbed pale cladding, graphite structural frame, a very wide
   full-height door on one end for finished craft to leave through. The
   hero building of the site.
2. **Parts factory** — 120 × 120 m. Houses twelve fabrication machines
   in bays. Lower and squarer than the ship factory, with a saw-tooth or
   ribbed roof reading as a row of machine bays, and modest goods doors
   rather than one huge one.
3. **Power plant** — 120 × 120 m. Feeds the whole site's electrical grid
   from eight generator units. Blocky housings in a row, heavy cooling
   structures, cable trunking leaving toward the site, and the strongest
   emissive trim of any building — this is the one that visibly hums.

Unlocked later, drawn now for the padlocked state:

4. **Receiving dock** — 80 × 60 m. Where bought goods arrive. A canopy
   over several vehicle bays, roller doors, hardstanding apron.
5. **Storage warehouse** — 100 × 80 m. High-bay automated storage. Tall,
   windowless, plain, the most anonymous building on the site — its job
   is to look like volume.
6. **Drone depot** — 60 × 60 m. Where drones charge and are repaired.
   Open-sided canopy over a grid of charging pads, each pad ringed with
   cool-blue light; several drones docked.
7. **Launch pad** — 120 × 120 m. Where finished craft depart, and the
   moment the whole game builds toward. A circular hardstand with a
   blast apron, low perimeter light masts, and heavy ground hazard
   striping. Keep it low and open so nothing hides the craft.
8. **Research lab** — 80 × 80 m. Cleaner and more precise than the rest —
   smoother cladding, finer emissive lines, a low domed or louvred roof
   element. The one building that looks like thinking rather than
   making.
9. **Test hall** — 100 × 80 m. Where finished craft are tested before
   delivery. Like a small ship factory but sealed and heavier, with
   thick doors and visible venting.
10. **Operations building** — 60 × 40 m. Contracts and customers. The
    smallest and most refined; a compact pale block with a strong
    emissive edge line. Still no windows implying floors of people.
11. **Materials refinery** — 100 × 100 m. Turns raw material into stock
    for the parts factory. The most industrial of the set: silos,
    pipework, a tall process tower, conveyors feeding in.
12. **Heavy ship factory** — 220 × 220 m. The late-game hall for the
    largest craft. Unmistakably the biggest thing on the site — same
    language as the ship factory but taller, with a deeper gantry
    structure and a colossal end door.

## What I need back

Twelve PNGs with alpha, one per building, same angle and same light,
drawn to the relative scale of the footprints above. If anything in the
brief is ambiguous, choose the reading that keeps the twelve looking
like one estate rather than twelve individual designs.
