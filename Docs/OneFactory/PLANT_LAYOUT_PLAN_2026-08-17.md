### Uncertainties I could not close

**U‑1 — The brief's density baselines.** Paint 0.0094 and assembly 0.013 per m² are not reproducible from instances ÷ authored bay area, which give 0.0054 and 0.0028. Press's 0.069 is also not reproducible: 4092 over the main body 220 × 120 m gives 0.155, and over the outlier bounds 265 × 205 m gives 0.075. I used building internal floor area throughout §5.1 and gave raw counts alongside so nothing depends on the ratio, but **if the brief's denominator is a project convention I have not found, the targets in §5.1 need rescaling by the same factor** and the ordering between shops does not change.

**U‑2 — Vendor mesh bounds are unread.** Booth, oven, deck, platform and window scales in §2.3 and §2.4 are specified from the process, not measured from the `.uasset` bounds, which I could not read from disk. **Every vendor mesh in this plan needs one calibration pass in the editor** before placement counts are trusted. This mostly affects the paint booth interior kit (`SM_PaintBoxWall_01‑04`, `Ceiling`, `Floor`), the platform family, and `SM_LargeWindowFramed`. It does not affect any authored `SM_LB_*` dimension or any coordinate in §2.

**U‑3 — Assembly footprint Y is a judgement, not a measurement.** 15000 comes from nine bands I chose (§2.4), the binding one being a 2500 south service band that can hold a 10‑position engine dress line. At the reviewed 950 cm that band cannot, and the powertrain sub‑assembly has to leave the building. I chose to grow the building rather than exile the sub‑assembly, because a GA shop whose engines arrive from off‑site reads as incomplete. **If 15000 is refused, the fallback is 12000 with AS6 moved to a south‑wall annexe** — state which.

**U‑4 — Paint's 913 m of process is my estimate.** It is built from the zone lengths in §2.3, which are conventional rather than derived from a Line Boss contract (only the ED line's 189 m is contractual). If the owner wants a shorter paint process — fewer flash tunnels, one base coat instead of two — paint drops toward 700 m and three lanes in 240 m would carry it. **The 300 m recommendation is sensitive to this number and to nothing else.**

**U‑5 — Two overlaps I resolved by moving content, and someone should sanity‑check the choice.** The press LOGISTICS decal (world Y 9600…10700) overlaps PTD's foundation band, and I moved the decal rather than the train. The 10 press west‑apron floor actors sit 1100 cm outside the new west wall, and I clipped them rather than growing press by 1500. Both are cheap either way; I picked the option that leaves authored machine content untouched.

**U‑6 — Meshy remediation is larger than any per‑shop design said, and I have not verified the full list.** The count is **36 poisoned paths on disk, not 16.** Beyond the three meshes and four materials already named, the folder `Candidates/PressShop/PR009_PR010_UserMeshy_v864` — whose *name* is itself poison, so everything inside inherits it — contains `SM_CA_MW_PR009_TexturedVisual_v861`, `SM_CA_MW_PR010_TexturedVisual_v861`, six textures (`T_PR009/PR010_BaseColor_v864`, `_Normal_v868`, `_ORM_v868`), plus `Image_0‑3` and `Material_0`. There are also 7 dev/validation maps that must never be cooked, and a hardcoded source string `/Game/LineBoss/Candidates/PaintShop/Meshy/SM_Test`. **The fix is a rename‑and‑reparent of paths and folders plus deleting the dev maps — the geometry is fine, only the strings are poison.** Two assets the site design will want, `SM_CA_MW_InboundLorry_Approved_v006` and `SM_CA_MW_AdjustableCoilStand_Approved_v005`, are poisoned by their bound `MeshyPBR` materials and **must not be placed in a shipping map until those materials are renamed.** Not blocking while gameplay is deferred; blocking the moment the bootstrap provenance guard runs.

**U‑7 — Press's transplant depends on the robot fix landing first.** The reference's dressed footprint measures 236 × 189 m only because of the 24 misplaced S07 links (PTD's vacuum cups reach reference Y 12945, 6945 cm outside the south wall, and 9 of the 24 are outside the shell entirely). **Fix them before any transplant**, or press content overflows a 12500‑deep building in Y and the whole footprint argument in §1.2 collapses. This is the one Phase 0 item with no fallback.

**U‑8 — Three claims in the per‑shop designs I could not independently confirm and have carried forward as stated.** The `AssemblyLineNativeKit_v001` tri counts (672–1704); the exact 597‑instance / 29‑batch weld presentation figure; and the 18/18 contact‑FK validation. None of them changes a coordinate — they affect only M31.1's scope and C4's risk. Worth a five‑minute check before Phase 3.

### Two things I want to be explicit about

**This plan changes code in seven places (C1–C7) and two test files, and there is no version of "get the plant running fully" that does not.** The authored bays cannot hold their own contents, the press reference is placed under a wrong rotation about a wrong datum, paint's frozen presentation asserts an exact 119‑item list, and assembly's asserts 24 identical carriers where three carrier systems are needed. Every per‑shop design tried to design around one or more of these and every review caught it. The honest sequence is: ratify the footprints, land C1–C5 as mechanical edits with their co‑edits, get C6/C7 signed off, then build content.

**The largest single risk is not authoring capacity — it is paint's contract.** Weld and assembly can be built to press parity with owned stock plus the conveyance kit and about eight new machines each. Paint needs twenty new machines, a scale correction, a forty‑anchor layout re‑issue, and the reversal of an explicitly approved design direction. If O‑2 is refused, paint stays a black box and the plant's answer to "is it running" will always be "three shops and a closed door."

ENVELOPE CONFLICT - I designed the ledger to the AUTHORED BAYS, not the owner's master plan, but the ledger exposes a real problem in Paint. The authored bays hold today's measured station extents with room to spare (press bay 320 m vs 260 m used; body 180 vs 160; assembly 280 vs 242). Paint is the exception: the bay is 220 m long and the code's own ED line is 189 m (six 18 m tanks + 9 m drain + 72 m oven), which consumes 86% of the bay before pretreatment, sealer, primer, topcoat, clear, oven 2 and polish are placed - a real paint process is 500-600 m of line. So the authored 2x2 arrangement is viable for press/body/assembly and NOT viable for paint at real scale. The owner's master plan (paint 400 m) is right about paint and the 2x2 is right about the site. Recommendation for the other four designs: keep the 2x2 serpentine and the authored press/body/assembly bays, and serpentine the paint line back on itself inside the paint bay (two 100 m passes under one roof) rather than resize the envelope. ## GENERAL ASSEMBLY (MOORCROSS WORKS ASSEMBLY BAY, TRIM / CHASSIS / FINAL)

DESIGNED TO: the authored 28000 x 12000 cm bay at centre (16500, 8500) — X 2500..30500, Y 2500..14500 — NOT the owner's 450 x 200 m master-plan shop. Reason, stated so the owner can overrule: (a) the 24 canonical station transforms in LBOneFactoryAssemblyStarterLayout.cpp CanonicalLocation() are inside a validated contract — ValidateStarterLayout() asserts every station envelope IsInsideOrOn the bay from MakeMoorcrossWorksShellLayout(), asserts no two envelopes intersect, and asserts each inter-station route is <= 6500 cm; the presentation actor then asserts exactly 95 instances derived from those transforms. Widening the bay to 450 x 200 m invalidates the contract, its two test files and the runtime coordinator route. (b) The master plan is marked "TBC" by the owner. (c) The master-plan sizes are internally inconsistent with the 62000 x 31000 envelope: 200 m of assembly depth plus 225 m of weld depth is 425 m against a 310 m envelope, so the four master-plan shops cannot coexist in the current site at all. (d) At the reference press shop's measured density (4092 actors over 265 x 205 m = 0.075 actors/m2) the authored bay wants ~2500 actors, which is buildable; 450 x 200 m wants ~6700 actors for one department, more than the whole reference shop. GROWTH PATH if the owner insists on 450 m: the fix is to unfold the serpentine into one straight leg by re-issuing CanonicalLocation() as X = 2000 + (n-1)*1850 at a single Y — a one-function change plus a bay resize — but the bay must then be 46000 x 8000 and the other three shops have to leave the 2x2.

LAYOUT. The frozen contract already describes a textbook GA shop, so the design stands content around it rather than re-planning it. Two aisles, a serpentine with the U-turn at the east end: leg A carries stations 1-12 at Y = 5500 running east (X 4000 to 28200 on a 2200 cm pitch); leg B carries stations 13-24 at Y = 11500 running west (X 28200 back to 4000). Station footprint is 1800 (X) x 3300 (Y), so leg A occupies Y 3850..7150 and leg B Y 9850..13150. That leaves five clean bands across the 12000 cm depth: south service band Y 2500..3850 (13.5 m), leg A, central spine Y 7150..9850 (27 m), leg B, north service band Y 13150..14500 (13.5 m). X margins are tight — 600 cm west, 1400 cm east — so the east margin becomes the U-turn transfer and the only wide shutter, and finished cars leave through the NORTH wall at station 24 (X 4000), not the west wall.

THE DESIGN'S SPINE IDEA, and the thing currently missing entirely: the shop must read as THREE different carrier systems, because that is what makes a GA shop legible. Stations 1-8 (trim) are a floor-level skillet line — a moving walking platform the operators ride, body at knee height. Station 9 lifts the body off the skillet onto an ELEVATED overhead chassis carrier so operators can work underneath through stations 10-14, and the powertrain comes up from beneath on decking pallets at the marriage station. Station 17 fits wheels, so from 17 onward the car is on its own tyres on a flat-top or drives itself. Right now all 24 positions carry the same SM_LB_Assembly_SkilletCarrier_v001 at the same height, which is why the shop reads as one repeated cell. Everything else follows: the door line is an overhead loop that leaves station 2, climbs to a mezzanine deck over the central spine (there is no mezzanine anywhere in the project, and the vendor SM_IndustrialPlatform / SM_PlatformGrill / SM_PlatformRailing / SM_FloorStairs families are sitting unused), builds doors in 8 positions, and drops them back at station 16 for refit — an overhead loop crossing the central aisle in front of the camera. ## PAINT

WHICH FOOTPRINT I DESIGNED TO, AND WHY. I designed to the AUTHORED bay: centre (10000,-8500), 22000 x 10000 cm (220 x 100 m), X -1000..21000, Y -13500..-3500. Not the owner's 400 x 100 m master-plan shop. Reasons: (1) LBOneFactoryPaintStarterLayout.cpp:531-536 hard-fails any station envelope that leaves GetPaintBayBounds(), which reads MakeMoorcrossWorksShellLayout - a 400 m design fails validation today; (2) in the 2x2 serpentine, paint's east edge is X=21000 and Assembly's bay starts at X=2500 - a 400 m paint shop reaches X=39000, past the 62000x31000 envelope and straight through Assembly; (3) depth is 100 m in BOTH, so nothing is lost there; (4) the master plan is marked TBC by the owner, the bay is code. Critically, the authored bay costs NO process content: my 5-lane serpentine delivers 925 m of line inside 220 m, more than a 400 m single-pass shop holds. THE ONE REAL ADVANTAGE OF THE MASTER PLAN, for the owner's decision: at 400 m the ED line's 189 m fits in ONE straight pass, so the six open dip tanks and the 72 m oven read as one continuous line rather than one lane of a fold. That is a visual argument, not a capacity one.

THE GEOMETRY. Paint is the emptiest shop because someone tried to fit a paint shop into one straight 118 m line. It does not fit: LBECoatLineActor.h alone contracts 18900 cm (six 1800 cm tanks + 900 cm drain + 7200 cm oven), and that is only pretreat + e-coat. A full paint shop needs ~900 m. So the line FOLDS. Five east-west lanes at 1650 cm pitch, centred on the existing Y=-8500 so the middle lane keeps the frozen station Y: L5 Y=-5200 (north), L4 Y=-6850, L3 Y=-8500, L2 Y=-10150, L1 Y=-11800 (south). Process run X 900..19300 (184 m per lane). West cross-aisle X -700..900; east cross-aisle + Painted Body Store X 19300..20700. South plant strip Y=-13050 (900 deep); north service strip Y=-4000 (1000 deep). Serpentine: BIW in from Weld through the WEST wall onto L5 (W->E, receipt/store/preclean); turn east onto L4 (E->W, the ED line - six OPEN dip tanks under a continuous overhead carrier gantry, then drain, then ONE long ENCLOSED 72 m oven, exactly the code contract, at authored 1:1 scale); turn west onto L3 (W->E, ED sand, sealer/underbody robots, gel oven, primer booth, primer flash); turn east onto L2 (E->W, primer oven, scuff deck, tack-off, base 1, flash, base 2, clearcoat airlock); turn west onto L1 (W->E, clearcoat booth, flash, 48 m topcoat oven, cooling, polish deck, inspection light tunnel, rework spur); then east to a 3-level elevated Painted Body Store and an overhead dispatch conveyor at Z=1300 flying north over L4/L5 and out of the NORTH wall at X~20200 to Assembly across the spine yard at Y=-1000. Direction parity checks out: 3 turns at the east aisle, 2 at the west. The ED line is the only process that overruns its lane - it runs X 19300 down to X 400, borrowing the west vestibule, which is precisely why the footprint question matters.

Its "six tanks" are the 1800 cm OpenTreatmentModule at TreatmentScaleX = 0.18 - i.e. 324 cm tanks on 324 cm centres. Its "long enclosed oven" is four 355 cm OvenSegment_v002 bays = 1420 cm. Two concrete defects found: (a) SM_LB_EDLine_OpenTreatmentModule_NoRail_Middle_v002 EXISTS on disk but the presentation actor binds only Start and End, so no tank can ever be built to full 18 m length - that is a code fix, not a missing asset; (b) the whole line sits at X 0..11800 inside a bay spanning X -1000..21000, leaving 92 m of the bay empty at the east end.

THREE FROZEN CONTRACTS BLOCK THIS DESIGN AND NEED A v003. (1) STATION COUNT AND REACH: RequiredStationCount=8, RequiredConnectionCount=7, MaximumRouteLengthCm=2800. Eight stations at 28 m max hop span at most 196 m - one lane. The 46 process zones below need a v003 layout of ~40 station anchors, or the 8 stay as coarse zone anchors with detail in presentation. (2) THE ROBOT BAN: ValidatePresentationContract rejects any PresentationId containing ROBOT, WINDOW, SIDE_DOOR or INTERNAL (lines 1189-1192), and PAINT_LINE_NATIVE_KIT_UNREAL_IMPORT_LANE_v001.md freezes "no spray robots". (3) COUNTS: ExpectedInstanceCount=119, ExpectedComponentCount=24, RequiredDirectPresentationAssetCount=10 are all asserted. The density bar from Codex's protected press reference is 4092 actors over a 220 x 120 m floor = 0.155 actors/m2; paint's 22,000 m2 therefore targets ~3,400 actors. Paint has 119. genuinely is Meshy-generated and the project's policy is Blender-native only. The
guard is right and stays intact.

consistency is cosmetic and repathing mid-build would invalidate placements for no
visual gain. Recorded so it is a deliberate choice rather than drift.

## Weld: what the design changes, for the record

The current 18 positions put panel receipt in the south-WEST corner and the BIW
handoff in the north-WEST - **170 m of backhaul from paint**, whose west wall is at
X=-1000. Two 32 m rows plus a 24 m aisle eat 88 of the bay's 100 m, leaving 6 m at
each wall: no receiving lane, no service corridor, no subassembly space. That, not
the station count, is why weld reads sparse.

The re-layout keeps all 18 frozen positions, ids and programme order, re-running them
at 1800 cm pitch on a serpentine opening EAST so both heads sit nearest the press
exit and paint entry. Arithmetic is checked: pitch 1800 > footprint 1700 (no in-row
overlap), row separation 4200 > 3200 (no cross-row overlap), all links under the
frozen 6200 cm maximum, all transforms Z=0 and unit scale. It goes through
`MoveStation`, so it is a data re-seed, not a contract change.

## Correction: the arch family is NOT shop-scale

The design calls `SM_HeavyArch01-04` and `SM_LampArch01` "shop-scale gantries over
each line". They are **6.0 m and 5.9 m tall**. The weld shop's walls are **22 m**, so
these are *machine-scale* arches - right over a single cell, useless as a hall
gantry. Reaching shop scale would need roughly 3.5x uniform scaling, which would
thicken every member and read as a toy.

**Decision:** use the arch family for what it is - per-cell gantries over the 18
stations, which the shop genuinely lacks - and treat shop-scale overhead steelwork as
an authoring item rather than a reuse item. Added to the missing list as

ratios: a number quoted from reasoning rather than measured.

## Two placement traps to honour

`SM_FloorStairs01` (minZ -176.7) and `SM_ElectricalPanel_01` (minZ -79.4) both extend
below their origin. Placed at Z=0 they sink into the slab. Every placement script must
offset by -minZ, not assume floor pivots - the `SM_LB_*` family uses floor pivots, the
vendor pack does not.

# Weld fabric pass: attempted, rejected, reverted (2026-08-17)

Placed 282 fabric actors into the weld bay from the idle vendor kit - 40 mezzanine
decks, 40 pillars, 78 railings, 2 stair flights, 18 per-cell gantries, 38 clerestory
windows, 42 racking tiers, 24 switchboards - captured it, and **removed all 282**.

The capture is the reason. Every pitch was correct and nothing overlapped, but:

- The mezzanine read as a **flat blue ribbon**, not a raised walkway.
  `SM_IndustrialPlatform01` is a blue-tinted deck panel; 40 of them in two 3 m lanes
  across a 10 m-deep bay is geometrically a narrow strip, and with thin pillars it
  reads as painted floor rather than structure.
- The 18 gantries read as **small dark brackets**, not portals. `SM_HeavyArch01` is a
  bracket/half-arch, so 18 of them in a row look like flags on the floor.
- The racking read as **orange fencing** - `SM_StorageShelves*` stacked three tiers
  high does not resemble a marshalling rack at this framing.

## The mistake, precisely

I measured the kit's **bounds** and treated that as knowing the kit. Bounds tell you
pitch and whether things fit; they say nothing about whether an asset *reads as* the
thing you are using it for. Last tick's calibration correctly caught two wrong
dimensions and then I placed 282 actors without ever looking at a single one of these
meshes rendered.

**Rule for the remaining shops:** before designing placement around any vendor asset,
render it once - a thumbnail grid of the candidate kit, inspected - and only then
commit to a layout. The asset ledger's "unused: 764 meshes" is an inventory, not a
recommendation; unused sometimes means unsuitable.

## What this does not change

Press stands unaffected (5,089 mesh + 144 light actors, commissioning green at
278/278). The weld **re-layout** analysis still holds and is independent of the kit:
the 18 positions genuinely are misplaced, with the BIW handoff 170 m of backhaul from
paint, and the re-seed through `MoveStation` remains the right first weld change.
Do that before any more dressing.

# Weld re-layout LANDED (2026-08-17) — and why the pitch stayed at 2000

`CanonicalLocation` / `CanonicalRotation` now run the 18 positions on a serpentine
that opens EAST, so both the inbound and outbound heads sit nearest the press exit and
the paint entry instead of the BIW handoff sitting 170 m of backhaul away at the west
wall. Rows moved to Y -7000 and -11200. **Suite 278/278, 0 failed, 0 notRun.**

Only ONE test failed on the first attempt, not the four the plan feared - and it
found a design flaw in the study rather than a stale expectation:

`LineBoss.OneFactory.PlayerBuilder.BodyWeldLifecycleRobotProgrammeRollbackMoveWIPAndRemoval`
exercises `ExecuteUMGAction(4)`, the player's "move station east 1 m". At the study's
proposed **1800 pitch** that action consumes all 100 cm of slack over the 1700
footprint, neighbours touch, and the validator refuses the move. The study's
arithmetic ("pitch 1800 > footprint 1700, so no in-row overlap") was right about the
static layout and wrong about the game: the player can move things.

**So the pitch stays at 2000**, which leaves 300 cm and lets a one-metre nudge clear in
either direction. Nothing of value was lost - the depth saving that mattered comes from
the row separation, not the pitch. The run now starts at X -3050 rather than -3200,
because 9 positions at 2000 plus the footprint need 17700 of the bay's 18000 and the
old start put the west end 50 cm outside `BodyBayBounds()`.

**Lesson worth keeping:** a layout that satisfies static clearance can still be wrong,
because the player's own edit actions need slack too. Check the interactive envelope,
not just the placement envelope.

Not done for weld: the freed strips are still empty. The receiving lane, service
corridor and mezzanine need content, and the vendor kit was rejected for reading
wrongly - so those need either authored assets or a curated selection made after
inspecting rendered thumbnails.

# Vendor kit contact sheet: rendered and judged (2026-08-17)

Stood 24 candidate meshes in a 6-wide grid on the empty paint bay floor, photographed
them, and cleared them again. Method note: the bay-level framing was far too wide -
each asset was about 3% of frame height and unjudgeable. `Paint@0.16~24` at 2769 cm
is the useful contact-sheet distance. Grid placement reused the existing bay framing,
so no new camera tooling was needed.

**The kit is a mix, not a uniform rejection.** Correcting my earlier blanket verdict:

**Usable as-is:**
- `SM_LampArch01` - a genuine slender lit gantry arch with a lamp head. Reads
  correctly and is the best thing in the kit. Use it for the per-cell gantries
  instead of `SM_HeavyArch01`.
- `SM_FloorStairs01` - proper stairs with visible treads and a side stringer. Good.
- `SM_ElectricalPanel_01`, `SM_ElectricalSupply_Switchboard01`, `SM_FloorDrainage01`,
  `SM_Container01_01` - small detail props, fine at their own scale.

**Not usable without work:**
- `SM_IndustrialPlatform01/02/03` - thin flat plates. At deck height with thin
  pillars they read as painted floor, which is exactly what the reverted fabric pass
  showed. A convincing mezzanine needs the deck, grill, railing and pillars
  assembled as one unit with a fascia, not decks scattered on a pitch.
- `SM_StorageShelves*` - perforated panels that read as fencing rather than racking.
- `SM_HeavyArch01/02/03` - brackets, not portals. Superseded by `SM_LampArch01`.

**Consequence for weld dressing:** the per-cell gantries can be built from owned
assets after all, by swapping `SM_HeavyArch01` for `SM_LampArch01`. The mezzanine and
the marshalling racks stay on the authoring list. That is a smaller authoring bill
than the earlier blanket rejection implied, and it is the second time today a blanket
verdict turned out too coarse - first "the bays cannot hold their contents", now "the
vendor kit reads wrongly".
